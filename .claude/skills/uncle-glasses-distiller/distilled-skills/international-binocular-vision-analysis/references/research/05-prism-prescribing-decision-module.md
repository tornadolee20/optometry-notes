# Prism Prescribing Decision Module

## Purpose

This module translates binocular vision findings into a structured prism-prescribing thought process.

It does not assume prism is always indicated.
Its job is to answer:

- when prism belongs in the conversation
- which rule is only supportive
- when prism is likely too simplistic an answer
- how to separate exo-driven, eso-driven, and fixation-disparity-driven logic

## First Principle

Prism is not prescribed because a rule exists.
Prism is considered when:

- symptoms are meaningful
- the binocular vision pattern is coherent
- the measured stress is plausibly related to the symptom
- red flags have been screened

## Stepwise Prism Logic

### Step 1: Confirm the Pattern

Before any prism logic, confirm whether the case behaves most like:

- exophoria / convergence insufficiency pattern
- esophoria / convergence excess pattern
- basic exo / basic eso
- decompensating heterophoria
- intermittent tropia / manifest deviation
- non-binocular driver masquerading as BV symptoms

If the pattern is unclear, do not jump to prism.

### Step 2: Confirm Symptom Relevance

Prism belongs in the discussion when symptoms are:

- task-linked
- reproducible
- binocularly plausible
- not better explained by refractive, ocular surface, neurologic, or systemic causes

### Step 3: Match the Rule to the Pattern

#### Exo-side logic

Most useful supports:

- Sheard's criterion
- PFV stress
- near symptom pattern
- CI-like pattern coherence
- fixation disparity / associated phoria when available

Interpretation:

- Sheard can support prism thinking in symptomatic exophoria
- low PFV relative to demand strengthens the case
- fixation disparity tools may help when the symptom pattern is real-world and not fully captured by dissociated tests

#### Eso-side logic

Most useful supports:

- NFV stress
- task demand
- accommodation-vergence relationship
- sometimes Percival as a secondary conceptual aid

Interpretation:

- eso prism logic should not rely on Percival alone
- always check accommodation links and near-task burden
- be cautious in pediatric, presbyopic, and special-population cases

### Step 4: Decide the Role of Fixation Disparity

Fixation disparity / associated phoria is most useful when:

- symptoms are real and sustained
- dissociated findings do not fully explain discomfort
- you suspect decompensation under natural binocular conditions

It is weakest when:

- used as a stand-alone prescribing tool
- disconnected from symptom logic
- used without understanding adaptation and variability

### Step 5: Decide Whether Prism Is Primary, Secondary, or Not Indicated

#### Primary consideration

Prism may be a primary option when:

- symptoms are significant
- pattern is coherent
- reserve stress is clear
- no red flags dominate the picture

#### Secondary consideration

Prism may be secondary when:

- pattern is present but not complete
- other management options may address the root driver
- the patient context suggests caution with adaptation or overcorrection

#### Not indicated yet

Avoid premature prism when:

- findings are inconsistent
- symptoms are weak or non-specific
- red flags are unresolved
- the problem looks more accommodative, ocular-surface, neurologic, or developmental than prism-responsive

## Exo vs Eso Board Logic

### Exophoria / CI-like Cases

Best-supported prism thought sequence:

1. establish exo-related symptom pattern
2. confirm near stress and pattern coherence
3. review PFV stress
4. use Sheard as support, not command
5. consider fixation disparity / associated phoria if needed

### Esophoria / CE-like Cases

Best-supported prism thought sequence:

1. establish eso-related task burden
2. review NFV stress
3. inspect accommodation contribution
4. treat Percival as optional support only
5. avoid formula-driven prescribing when the clinical picture is incomplete

## Confidence Levels for Prism Thinking

### High confidence prism conversation

- symptom pattern is strong
- binocular findings are coherent
- rule support and physiologic stress point in same direction
- no major red flags are present

### Moderate confidence prism conversation

- probable pattern is present
- some but not all supporting findings align
- more testing or trial framing is needed

### Preliminary only

- pattern is incomplete
- rule support is weak or contradictory
- method noise may explain the finding

## What Not to Do

- do not prescribe from Sheard alone
- do not prescribe from Percival alone
- do not treat associated phoria as universal truth
- do not ignore adaptation, age, task context, or special-population modifiers
