# Special Populations Module: Concussion and Pediatric

## Purpose

This module tracks binocular vision logic that may differ from the general adult symptomatic clinic population.

The goal is to prevent overgeneralization.

Not every binocular vision finding from:

- concussion clinics
- pediatric populations
- developmental or educational settings

should be transferred directly into the general adult framework.

## Population A: Concussion / TBI

### Why It Needs Its Own Layer

Post-concussion binocular vision complaints often include:

- near blur
- diplopia
- headaches
- difficulty sustaining reading
- visual motion discomfort
- reduced endurance

These cases may resemble classic CI or accommodative dysfunction, but the pathway is not always identical to routine primary binocular vision dysfunction.

### What to Preserve From the Main Skill

- symptom-guided assessment
- four-domain analysis
- red-flag escalation logic
- pattern-based interpretation

### What to Keep in Evolving Edge

- whether concussion CI behaves identically to primary CI
- whether therapy response patterns are fully transferable
- whether certain test priorities should be reordered in concussion settings

### Current Working Rule

Use the main binocular vision framework first, but add caution before assuming:

- same etiology
- same prognosis
- same treatment response

## Population B: Pediatric

### Why It Needs Its Own Layer

Children are not just smaller adults.

Interpretation changes because of:

- developmental stage
- cooperation variability
- language and attention factors
- school-demand context
- amblyopia / strabismus overlap

### What Must Change in Pediatric Logic

- age matters more when reading norms
- symptom reporting is less reliable
- parent / teacher observations matter more
- manifest deviation and sensory development issues carry higher structural weight

### Pediatric Priority Signals

Pay special attention to:

- reading avoidance
- short visual stamina
- intermittent eye turn
- abnormal head posture
- poor stereopsis
- suppression concerns
- school-function complaints that do not fit simple refractive error

### Current Working Rule

For pediatric cases:

1. do not over-rely on self-reported symptoms
2. separate developmental / strabismic concerns from pure functional BV fatigue
3. interpret cutoffs with age-aware caution

## How to Use This Module

When a new paper or guideline concerns a special population, ask:

1. Does it modify the `Stable Core`, or only the subgroup branch
2. Can the findings be generalized to routine adult BV practice
3. Does it affect test choice, interpretation, or management expectations

## Open Questions

1. Which concussion findings truly justify a distinct diagnostic pathway
2. Which pediatric binocular vision cutoffs are the most clinically predictive rather than merely statistically different
3. How best to separate educational-performance complaints from binocular dysfunction, accommodation dysfunction, or broader neurodevelopmental factors
