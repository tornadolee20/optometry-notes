# Rory Sutherland Research Notes

Updated: 2026-04-14

## Why Rory Matters Here

Rory Sutherland is unusually useful for Uncle Glasses because he sits at the intersection of:
- behavioral science
- marketing and trust-building
- perceived value and pricing
- framing, signaling, and reducing psychological friction

That makes him especially relevant to:
- progressive lenses and myopia control conversations
- explaining premium options without sounding salesy
- helping customers and families cross the gap between "I understand" and "I am willing"
- designing retail experiences where reassurance is part of the product

## Distilled Mental Models

### 1. Psycho-logic beats narrow logic
Humans do not buy, comply, or decide by spreadsheet alone. The emotionally sensible answer may outperform the mechanically rational one.

### 2. Perceived value is real value
Waiting, reassurance, ceremony, explanation, and status all change experienced utility.

### 3. Reframing is often cheaper than rebuilding
Sometimes the highest-leverage improvement is not more technology but a better story, sequence, expectation, or default.

### 4. Tiny contextual changes can create outsized effects
The highest ROI often comes from seemingly small details in wording, timing, ordering, or social proof.

### 5. Signaling matters because humans infer quality indirectly
People use cues, rituals, and design signals to judge competence and safety when they cannot directly judge technical quality.

### 6. Creativity is a behavioral advantage, not mere decoration
Creative framing can unlock behavior that operational efficiency alone cannot reach.

## Heuristics for Optometry Translation

### 1. Sell the felt improvement, not just the lens specification
Translate technology into fewer headaches, smoother transitions, more confidence driving, easier reading, less family worry.

### 2. Remove adaptation anxiety before discussing price
Uncertainty often blocks commitment more than cost does.

### 3. Explain premium options as risk reduction and life quality
Do not frame them as "upgrades" only; frame them as removing friction from real daily moments.

### 4. Treat scripts and follow-up as product design
The sentence after the exam can matter as much as the exam.

### 5. Create rituals that signal care and precision
Measurement flow, handoff language, fitting explanation, and post-purchase follow-up all create belief.

### 6. Look for inexpensive contextual wins
Examples:
- adaptation cards
- family-facing explanation sheets
- a better waiting explanation
- a "what to expect in week 1" message

## Expression DNA

Rory's style tends to be:
- playful but precise
- contrarian without being nihilistic
- rich in examples and analogies
- skeptical of simplistic efficiency metrics
- enthusiastic about weird but effective solutions
- fond of memorable phrases and surprising comparisons

## Boundaries and Risks

- Rory is not a clinical authority; his value here is decision design, communication, and perceived value.
- His lens should not override medical accuracy or ethical limits.
- In this workspace, Rory should complement Atul Gawande and Feynman rather than replace them.

Best blend:
- Atul for care and humane judgment
- Feynman for clear explanation
- Rory for framing, adoption, and customer psychology
