## Career and Positioning Decisions

### 1. Staying inside Ogilvy while building a behavioral science practice

This is an important strategic signal.
Rory did not isolate behavioral science into purely academic commentary.
He embedded it inside a real commercial system.

What this suggests:
- he values application over purity
- he wants insight to change actual decisions
- he believes creativity and behavioral science belong together

### 2. Hosting and building Nudgestock

Nudgestock is effectively a decision to create a recurring forum for:
- cross-disciplinary idea exchange
- real-world behavioral cases
- legitimacy for behavioral thinking inside business

What this suggests:
- Rory thinks communities and repeated rituals matter
- he builds intellectual infrastructure, not just speeches
- he understands that ideas spread better through events and culture, not only books

### 3. Writing for broad audiences

He writes and speaks in public-facing formats rather than retreating into specialist language.

What this suggests:
- he values transmissibility
- he wants ideas to survive contact with real people
- he optimizes for adoption, not just correctness

## Recurring Decision Logic

### Logic 1: Prefer asymmetric interventions

Rory is drawn to actions where:
- cost is small
- upside is large
- the market underestimates the mechanism

### Logic 2: Prefer hidden levers over visible effort

He repeatedly looks for:
- script changes
- choice architecture
- expectation shifts
- social signals
- design cues

This suggests a decision style tuned to leverage, not heroics.

### Logic 3: Prefer ideas that align with real human behavior

He does not appear interested in forcing people to act like ideal rational agents.
He appears more interested in designing with actual human tendencies.

## Decision Heuristics Derived from Rory

1. If the obvious solution is expensive, ask whether the real problem is psychological.
2. If adoption is weak, inspect the frame before rebuilding the engine.
3. If quality is hard to verify, design better quality signals.
4. If people delay action, reduce ambiguity before adding pressure.
5. If a choice feels too abstract, anchor it in a lived moment.
6. If customers are confused, the system is probably over-explaining the wrong thing.
