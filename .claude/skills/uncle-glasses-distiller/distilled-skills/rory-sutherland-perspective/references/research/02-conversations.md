# 02 Conversations

## Public Talk Patterns

### 1. He answers business questions as human behavior questions

In Rory's public material, the pattern is consistent:
- when others discuss efficiency, he asks what humans notice
- when others discuss performance, he asks what humans infer
- when others discuss price, he asks what humans fear

This is his central move in conversation: translating operational questions into psychological ones.

### 2. He likes examples more than abstraction

Rory rarely argues in purely theoretical language for long.
He quickly moves to:
- transport and waiting examples
- pricing and choice examples
- brand signaling examples
- absurd but memorable comparisons

Function of this style:
- makes counterintuitive points easier to accept
- lowers resistance
- lets the audience "feel" the point before they fully analyze it

### 3. He uses humor to smuggle in serious ideas

He often sounds playful, but the humor is carrying a real challenge:
- your metrics are too narrow
- your assumptions about rationality are too simplistic
- your model of value is incomplete

Humor works for him because it keeps the audience open while he attacks orthodoxy.

## Interview-Level Thought Habits

### Habit 1: Re-describe the problem

He often improves a question by renaming it.

Examples of the pattern:
- from "How do we sell this?" to "Why doesn't the existing setup make sense to humans?"
- from "How do we improve the product?" to "How do we improve the experience of believing in the product?"
- from "Why are people irrational?" to "What hidden logic are they using?"

### Habit 2: Look for neglected variables

He pays attention to:
- waiting
- uncertainty
- script wording
- defaults
- ceremony
- social proof
- symbols of competence

These are not side issues for him; they are often the main event.

### Habit 3: Ask what customers cannot directly evaluate

When technical quality is hard to judge, people infer quality from proxies.

That means:
- explanation style matters
- environment matters
- staff confidence matters
- sequencing matters
- tone matters

This is extremely relevant to any expert service business.

## Conversation Takeaways for Optometry

### When discussing premium lenses

Do not start with:
- index
- coating complexity
- design architecture

Start with:
- what daily friction gets removed
- what discomfort gets prevented
- what mental load becomes lighter
- what regret becomes less likely

### When discussing adaptation

Do not treat adaptation as a disclaimer only.

Treat it as:
- expectation design
- uncertainty reduction
- meaning assignment
- trust maintenance

### When discussing price

Do not let price appear before psychological value is legible.
Once people understand the felt benefit and reduced risk, price is processed differently.

## Signature Conversational Moves

1. Challenge the obvious answer.
2. Introduce an invisible psychological factor.
3. Make it vivid through analogy.
4. Turn the insight into a practical implication.

That is the conversational engine to preserve in the skill.
