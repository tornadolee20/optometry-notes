# distillation-source-strategy.md

Updated: 2026-04-15

## Purpose

This reference defines how the system should think about source quality before distilling a target into a reusable asset.

## Core Rule

Do not build a reusable skill from weak, derivative, or hype-distorted material if better source categories are available.

## Source Priority

Prefer, in order:

1. primary materials
2. direct interviews / speeches / writings
3. documented decisions / case evidence
4. high-quality secondary synthesis
5. tertiary commentary only as support

## Person Distillation

Best source categories:

- direct talks or interviews
- letters, essays, books, or public writing
- decision records
- credible biographical reconstruction

Be careful when:

- the person is mostly known through fan interpretation
- the internet repeats slogans instead of decisions
- quotes are detached from context

## Topic Distillation

Best source categories:

- foundational papers
- official guidance
- authoritative reviews
- respected field explanations
- repeatable real-world operating patterns

Be careful when:

- the topic is fashionable but not yet stable
- consensus is weak
- source quality varies wildly

## Medical / Technical Topics

Raise the quality bar when:

- the content influences care decisions
- the topic is clinical
- the wording may imply evidence strength

Prefer:

- guidelines
- systematic reviews
- strong domain references

Avoid distilling from:

- low-quality summaries alone
- unsourced social claims
- overconfident interpretations of narrow evidence

## Hype Protection

The more hyped a topic is, the more the system should ask:

- what is signal versus slogan?
- what are the actual operating patterns?
- what would remain true if the hype disappeared?

## Decision Rule

If the sourcing base is weak:

- choose `research-first`
- or `note-first`

If the sourcing base is strong and the recurring value is clear:

- `framework-first`
- or `skill-first`

becomes more justified.

## Bottom Line

Distillation quality is capped by source quality.

Strong assets come from strong source discipline.
