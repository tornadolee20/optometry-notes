## Study Type

Narrative / clinical review.

This is not a primary pediatric spectacle-lens visual-performance trial. Its value for `DQ-KDF-001-002` is conceptual framing: myopia control decisions should consider more than efficacy.

## Extracted Abstract Signal

The review states that multiple myopia-control modalities can achieve clinically meaningful efficacy, including some spectacle lenses, selected soft contact lenses, atropine concentrations, and overnight orthokeratology.

Because satisfactory efficacy can be achieved through multiple options, the authors emphasize four additional clinical decision factors:

1. compliance
2. quality of vision
3. quality of life
4. safety

The abstract specifically notes that compliance directly impacts efficacy regardless of modality, and that compliance is influenced by quality of vision and comfort.

## Relevance to DQ-KDF-001-002

This source supports the *framing* of the discovery question:

> Efficacy and central visual acuity are not sufficient by themselves when selecting or monitoring myopia-control interventions. Quality of vision, comfort, quality of life, safety, and compliance should also be considered.

For the KDF thread, this strengthens the rationale for asking whether standard central visual acuity is enough to represent real-world use.

## What It Supports

Allowed cautious uses:

- Myopia-control care should consider factors beyond efficacy.
- Compliance matters because real-world treatment effect depends on actual use.
- Quality of vision and comfort can influence compliance.
- The `DQ-KDF-001-002` focus on visual comfort, real-world task performance, and adherence is clinically reasonable.

## What It Does Not Prove

Do not use this paper to claim:

- Near-sightedness control spectacle lenses cause real-world activity problems.
- Standard central visual acuity fails for every child.
- Any specific spectacle-lens design is more or less comfortable than another.
- Pediatric spectacle-lens users have worse quality of life than other groups.
- Field observation scores are validated clinical outcomes.
