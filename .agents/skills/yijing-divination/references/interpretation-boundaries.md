# Interpretation Boundaries

Use this file when confidence, source integrity, or historical precision matters.

## Non-Negotiables

- Never invent classical wording.
- Never imply certainty where the source tradition is layered or disputed.
- Never merge original text, commentary, and modern advice without labels.

## Label Types Clearly

Mark statements as one of:

- `textual`: directly grounded in the hexagram or line text
- `traditional inference`: grounded in transmitted commentary or established interpretive tradition
- `modern application`: present-day extrapolation
- `uncertain`: disputed, school-specific, or historically unclear

## Historical Caution

Be careful with claims such as:

- `this is exactly how the Zhou people did it`
- `this line definitively means X in all schools`
- `the transformed hexagram overrides the original text`

These claims are often too strong.

## Practical Caution

Do not present a reading as:

- guaranteed prediction
- medical, legal, or financial certainty
- proof that one life path is cosmically fixed

## Better Framing

Prefer:

- `textually, the hexagram emphasizes...`
- `within the received interpretive tradition...`
- `as a modern application, this may suggest...`
- `this part is less certain because...`
